Recently, with advancement of large language models, new capabilities begin to emerge, for example, _zero-shot learning_ and also _few-shot learning_.

What is zero-shot learning? The model can predict the answer given only a description of the task. There's no gradient, no training. Given a new task, you just tell it what is the task, and then it's going to give you the answer. Say "translate from English to French" - we're not training a separate model for English to French or English to Chinese, just the same model using different prompts to describe the task. And zero-shot means there's no example - directly translate from "cheese" to French.

Also, the large language models have the capability called few-shot learning. We just give it a few examples of the task as part of the prompt engineering. They translate English to French, and then we are going to give it three examples, and then finally give the result to translate "cheese." So again, there's no gradient, there's no fine-tuning. I just design the prompt smartly, and the model is capable of doing such zero-shot or few-shot learning.
